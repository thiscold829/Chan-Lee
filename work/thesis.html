Master’s thesis: AI detection of food-safety signals in online text

A fine-tuned model extracts signals more accurately than a large language model at a fraction of the compute, and scores them in line with ratings from five experts.

ApproachResultsResearch since high schoolDocuments and screens
Where
Chung-Ang University, Food Safety and Regulatory Science. Advisor: Prof. Hyang Sook Chun
Period
September 2024 to August 2026
Role
Sole author
Deliverables
Thesis, fine-tuned models, a weighted risk index. Data: 856,336 online posts, January 2021 to November 2024
Thesis record, Chung-Ang University dCollection

Approach

Consumer posts carry the first signs of food-safety problems, but there are far too many to read. The thesis builds a pipeline that reads them at scale, extracts the signals, and ranks them the way experts would.

Four stages. Named-entity recognition finds five kinds of entities in each post. Sentiment analysis separates complaints from noise. A fine-tuned model extracts signals as sets of entities. A risk index then scores every signal on five indicators, weighted by a survey of experts.

Results

0.97F1, named-entity recognition, five entity types
0.95F1, sentiment analysis (lexicon baseline 0.49)
0.93F1, signal extraction (Llama 3.1 8B zero-shot 0.59)
31×less computation than the LLM baseline
0.86F1, agreement with five expert annotators
5risk indicators, weighted by expert survey

The fine-tuned model outperforms a large language model on signal extraction while using a fraction of the computation, and its scores agree with the panel of five experts.
