Decentralized Museum of Korea

An online museum of public-domain literature, art and music, open to anyone, anywhere. I set its name and principles and opened its three halls with the staff of each hall.

The museum

The Decentralized Museum of Korea is an online museum of public-domain literature, art and music, open for anyone to read, view and hear, wherever they are. ‘Decentralized’ means two things. The works belong to no nation or institution, so the museum holds them in trust rather than owning them. And instead of waiting for visitors to come, the museum goes to where they are.

The emblem is a red seal of the character 中, ‘centre’, with its central stroke taken out.

Visit the museum

Lobby

A seal is stamped as you enter. Scroll, and the window at its centre opens onto the inside of Seokguram Grotto. Three scenes, the chamber, the Buddha from the front and from the side, each carry one sentence; the signboards for the three halls and the visitor information follow.

Jikji Hall: literature

Jikji Hall is an e-book library. It holds classics of world literature and reference works in the original English, alongside the museum’s own Korean translations. Covers face out on the shelves; on a book’s page you can turn the book to see its spine and back. The reader turns pages like a printed book and keeps your place and bookmarks on your own device.

Each translation starts from a design document that fixes forms of address, register and terminology, and is rendered paragraph by paragraph against the original. Every departure from the original goes into a review table and is checked; only books that have been through this open in Korean.

Bangudae Hall: art

Bangudae Hall is a gallery of special exhibitions, each built around a theme and laid out room by room: The Whale-Road, whales through human eyes; Masterpieces for Everyone, works anyone can see and download to keep; Wherever They Are, Korean heritage scattered around the world, seen together. Choose a poster on the wall to see an exhibition and its rooms; inside, each work is shown large, one at a time, with a docent’s commentary.

Jongmyo Hall: music

Jongmyo Hall is a music hall organised as albums of Korean music, including the Jongmyo ritual music, and of Western classical music. Pick an album from the shelf and listen with its notes and track list.

Principles

Only works whose copyright term has ended under Korean law are shown. Each book’s colophon gives the life dates of its author, editor and illustrator and names its source texts; photographs that come with attribution terms are credited on those terms. No account is needed, and contact details appear in one place only: the director’s card.

How it is made

Each hall has its own AI staff: a curator and translators for Jikji Hall, curators for Bangudae and Jongmyo Halls, and an outreach lead, all working under the same rules. I decide the names, principles, screens and wording, and check the results. Anything that states a fact is backed by a source table first, and only what has been checked goes out.

On a phone

The phone layout is designed separately: the hall dock sits at the bottom, swipes follow the finger, and a double tap never zooms the page.
