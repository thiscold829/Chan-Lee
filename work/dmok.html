Decentralized Museum of Korea

An online museum where anyone, anywhere, can encounter the literature, art and music of all humanity. I set its name and principles and opened its three halls and the mountain hermitage Mumunam with the staff of each.

The museum

The Decentralized Museum of Korea is a museum where anyone, anywhere, can encounter the literature, art and music of all humanity. Decentralized means two things. The works belong to no nation or institution; the museum does not own them, but holds them in trust and puts them on view. And instead of asking visitors to come to it, the museum goes wherever its visitors are.

The emblem is a red seal of the character 中, ‘centre’, with its central stroke taken out.

Visit the museum

Lobby

At the entrance the museum’s red seal is stamped, and the way in passes the Seokguram Buddha to the signboards of the three halls. At the very end a back gate opens onto the mountain path up to Mumunam, the hermitage behind the museum.

Jikji Hall: literature

Jikji Hall is an e-book library of world literary classics. Every book is held in English; works first written in Russian, French, German or Italian also come in their original language, and the museum’s own Korean translations sit alongside. Every cover is the museum’s own design, and on a book’s page you can turn the book as if in your hand to see its spine and back. The Korean audiobooks mark each sentence as it is read, and your place and bookmarks stay on your device, so you can pick up where you left off.

Each translation starts from a design document that fixes forms of address, register and terminology, and is rendered paragraph by paragraph against the original. Every departure from the original goes into a review table and is checked; only books that have been through this open in Korean.

Bangudae Hall: art

Bangudae Hall is the museum’s art gallery. Its current special exhibitions are The Whale-Road, Masterpieces for Everyone and Wherever They Are. Each was curated in-house, with a theme, a choice of works and a sequence of rooms, and a docent’s commentary written for every work. Works are shown large, one at a time: read the commentary, or turn on the voice docent and follow it as it reads. Every exhibition poster is the museum’s own design.

Jongmyo Hall: music

Jongmyo Hall is a music hall organised as albums of Korean music, including the Jongmyo ritual music, and of Western classical music. Pick an album from the shelf and listen with its notes and track list.

Mumunam: a mountain hermitage

Mumunam (無門庵), ‘a hermitage without gates’, is an online hermitage on the mountain behind the museum, open to anyone without signing in. Its mountain path, one-pillar gate, courtyard and Dharma Hall are built in 3D to walk through. In the hall you can sit, bow, join your palms and listen to the chanting, and every day at 4 am, noon and 6 pm, Korean time, a service begins with the temple bell and chanting. The real time of day, weather and season show on the mountain, and others who are there at the same time can be seen. There is no chat.

Visit Mumunam

Principles

Only works that can be used free of any copyright problem are shown. Each book’s colophon gives the life dates of its author, editor and illustrator and names its source texts; photographs that come with attribution terms are credited on those terms. No account is needed, and contact details appear in one place only: the director’s card.

How it is made

Each hall has its own AI staff: a curator and translators for Jikji Hall, curators for Bangudae and Jongmyo Halls, a keeper for Mumunam and an outreach lead, all working under the same rules. I decide the names, principles, screens and wording, and check the results. Anything that states a fact is backed by a source table first, and only what has been checked goes out.

On mobile devices

Mobile devices get their own layout, designed around the movement of the finger.
