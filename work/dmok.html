Decentralized Museum of Korea

An online museum of public-domain literature, art and music, open to anyone, anywhere. I set its name and principles and built its three halls with AI.

The museum

The Decentralized Museum of Korea is an online museum of public-domain literature, art and music, open for anyone to read, view and hear, wherever they are. ‘Decentralized’ means two things. The works belong to no nation or institution, so the museum holds them in trust rather than owning them. And instead of waiting for visitors to come, the museum goes to where they are.

The emblem is a red seal of the character 中, ‘centre’, with its central stroke taken out.

Visit the museum

Lobby

A seal is stamped as you enter. Scroll, and the window at its centre opens onto the inside of Seokguram Grotto. Three scenes follow, each with one sentence: the whole chamber, a closer look up at the Buddha, and a view from the side, still looking up. The signboards for the three halls and the visitor information come after.

Jikji Hall: literature

Jikji Hall is an e-book library. You choose from shelves where covers face out; on a book’s page you can turn the book to see its spine and back. The reader turns pages like a printed book and keeps your place and bookmarks on your own device. Alongside the English original, readers can choose the museum’s own Korean translation.

Each translation starts from a design document that fixes forms of address, register and terminology, and is then rendered paragraph by paragraph against the original. Every place where the translation departs from the original goes into a review table that I approve item by item; only finished books open in Korean.

Bangudae Hall: art

Bangudae Hall is a gallery for special exhibitions. Choose a poster on the wall to see the exhibition and its rooms; inside, each work is shown large, one at a time, with a docent’s commentary. The first exhibition is The Whale-Road.

Jongmyo Hall: music

Jongmyo Hall presents music as albums. Pick a Korean or a Western classical album from the shelf and listen with its notes and track list.

Principles

Only works whose copyright term has ended under Korean law are shown. Each book’s colophon gives the life dates of its author, editor and illustrator and names its source texts; photographs that come with attribution terms are credited on those terms. No account is needed, and contact details appear in one place only: the director’s card.

How it is made

Each part of the museum has its own AI working session: the library, translation, exhibitions, music, the card and outreach, all under the same rules. I decide the names, principles, screens and wording, and check the results. Anything that states a fact is backed by a source table first, and only what has been checked goes out.

On a phone

The phone layout is designed separately: the hall dock sits at the bottom, swipes follow the finger, and a double tap never zooms the page.
