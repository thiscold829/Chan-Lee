The museum

The Decentralized Museum of Korea is a museum where anyone, anywhere, can encounter the literature, art and music of all humanity. Decentralized means two things. The works belong to no nation or institution; the museum does not own them, but holds them in trust and puts them on view. And instead of asking visitors to come to it, the museum goes wherever its visitors are.

The emblem is a red seal of the character 中, ‘centre’, with its central stroke taken out. It is stamped at the entrance; the way in passes the Seokguram Buddha to the signboards of the three halls, and a back gate at the very end opens onto the hermitage Mumunam.

Visit the museum

How it runs

One director runs the museum, from concept to daily operation, with a team of AI staff: a curator and translators for Jikji Hall, curators for Bangudae and Jongmyo Halls, a keeper for Mumunam and an outreach lead, all working under the same rules. I decide the names, principles, screens and wording, and review the results. Anything that states a fact is backed by a source table first, and only what has been checked goes out. News goes out on Instagram and YouTube, @dmok.museum.

A machine-made voice has to be declared as one, so the docent was given a name: the AI docent Nuri. When the outside archive holding the sound files closed its doors, the files moved to Sound Archives on GitHub, named after the Joseon dynasty’s archives, the sago. Readers’ suggestions brought in twentieth-century classics.

Jikji Hall: literature

Jikji Hall is an e-book library of literary classics, from the canon to the twentieth century. Every book is held in English, works first written in Russian, French, German or Italian also in the original, and many in the museum’s own Korean translation. Every cover is the museum’s own design. The AI docent Nuri reads the Korean editions aloud, marking each sentence; English and German books play recordings by human readers. With a sign-in, your place and bookmarks follow you across devices.

Each translation starts from a design document that fixes forms of address, register and terminology, and is rendered paragraph by paragraph against the original. Every departure from the original goes into a review table and is checked; only books that have been through this open in Korean.

Bangudae Hall: art

Bangudae Hall is the museum’s art gallery. Its current special exhibitions are The Whale-Road, Eyes on the Universe, Masterpieces for Everyone and Wherever They Are. Each was curated in-house, with a theme, a choice of works and a sequence of rooms, and a docent’s commentary written for every work. Every exhibition poster is the museum’s own design.

The AI docent Nuri reads each commentary aloud and lights up the sentence being read. When one work ends it moves on to the next, so a whole exhibition can be seen while listening.

Jongmyo Hall: music

Jongmyo Hall is a music hall organised as albums of Korean music, including the Jongmyo ritual music, and of Western classical music. Pick an album from the shelf and listen with its notes and track list.

Mumunam: a mountain hermitage

Mumunam (無門庵), ‘a hermitage without gates’, is an online hermitage on the mountain behind the museum, open to anyone. The mountain is built in 3D, and visitors walk it to rest, to stroll and to go adventuring.

Rest

In the hall you can sit, bow, join your palms and listen to the chanting, and every day at 4 am, noon and 6 pm, Korean time, a service begins with the temple bell and chanting.

Walk

From the courtyard to the mountain path and the lookout rock, the real time of day, weather and season show on the mountain. In winter, snow settles.

Adventure

Newcomers first have their head shaved and receive a Dharma name. Collecting seals along the pilgrimage path earns whale prayer beads, and with them a second pilgrimage opens: an offering carried deep into the forest.

Mumunam also has hidden places: a cliff above the sea, a whale you meet if you dive, a secret passage that opens when you strike the moktak, a dangerous underground dungeon and a deep forest where offerings are taken.

Together

Visitors there at the same time see one another through a real-time server; joining palms shows a nickname for a moment. With a sign-in, the walk carries on from another device.

Visit Mumunam

Principles

Only works that can be used free of any copyright problem are shown. Each book’s colophon gives the life dates of its author, editor and illustrator and names its source texts; photographs that come with attribution terms are credited on those terms. Everything can be seen without an account. Signing in is optional, and no email address or password is asked for.

On mobile devices

Mobile devices get their own layout, designed around the movement of the finger.
